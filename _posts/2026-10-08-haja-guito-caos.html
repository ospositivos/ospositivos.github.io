Societies become more complex as they try to solve problems [by] creating new layers of bureaucracy, infrastructure, or social class to address the challenge. Such complexity requires a substantial "energy" subsidy, meaning the consumption of resources, or other forms of wealth. Societies collapse when their investments in social complexity and their energy subsidies reach a point of diminishing marginal returns.
